- Petition gegen Beendigung. A serious-looking online petition demanding that the Littenstraße Baustelle not be completed, on grounds of cultural heritage. People can "sign" with their name and a one-line reason. The list of reasons becomes its own content — sincere absurdity. You're inverting the natural political stance (everyone hates ghost sites) which is exactly what makes the format funny. Each signature increments a public counter, which is its own viral mechanic.
- Denkmalschutz-Antrag. A downloadable, formally correct application for Denkmalschutz (heritage protection) for a Berlin ghost site. The argument, written deadpan: the fence has stood untouched for seven years, representing a unique form of preserved post-2019 urban administrative culture, and removing it now would erase an authentic historical layer. Make it look exactly like a real Antrag auf Eintragung in die Denkmalliste. People download it as a joke, but the format is so close to the real thing that some will half-seriously consider filing it. Either reaction is good.
- Selbstausdruckende Urkunde. A printable "Diplom" certifying a fence's age. The recipient enters the address and start date in a form; the page generates a PDF certificate styled exactly like a German Urkunde — gold border, gothic-ish heading, "Hiermit wird beurkundet, dass die Baustelle in der [Adresse] das Alter von [X] Jahren erreicht hat. Berlin, den [Datum]." With a fake official seal at the bottom. People print these and tape them to actual fences. Now you have physical artifacts in the wild photographed and posted by strangers. Cost: one Saturday with a PDF library.
